A production facility can be built in a couple of years. The scientists and operators to run it take longer, and that is where the Kingdom's attention has turned.

The national strategy targets 11,000 skilled biotech jobs by 2030. At September's Riyadh Global Medical Biotechnology Summit, which reported more than 40 agreements and announcements with a declared value above SAR 5 billion, several of the most practical ones were about people:

- Princess Nourah bint Abdulrahman University and Cellenkos launched a programme to develop Saudi women in cell and gene therapy and biomanufacturing.
- SVAX and Batterjee Medical College agreed accredited biomanufacturing training with up to 350 seats per cycle.
- The King Abdullah International Medical Research Center announced a centre of excellence for genomics, proteomics and biobanking with Thermo Fisher Scientific.

For us, capability building is not a side benefit of localisation. It is the condition for it. In every company we work with we ask the same questions. Who will be trained? Where, and by whom? Does the knowledge stay when the visiting team goes home?

A localisation plan that cannot answer those questions is a sales plan. One that can answer them is the beginning of an industry.
