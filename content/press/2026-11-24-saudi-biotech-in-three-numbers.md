Three numbers explain why biotech has moved up the agenda in Riyadh.

**3%.** The National Biotechnology Strategy, launched in January 2024, aims for biotech to contribute 3% of non-oil GDP by 2040, which the government puts at SAR 130 billion.

**11,000.** That is the number of skilled jobs the strategy targets by 2030, rising to 55,000 by 2040.

**Four.** The strategy concentrates on four areas: vaccines, biomanufacturing and localisation, genomics, and plant optimisation.

What sets this apart from most national plans is the demand side. The Kingdom is the customer as well as the sponsor. It runs a large public health system, it has a stated policy of making more of its medicines at home, and its regulator is moving faster. The Saudi Food and Drug Authority has reported an 83% rise in applications for advanced therapy and biotech clinical trials in 2025.

Our work at Taranis Capital sits on the supply side. We look for established biotech companies abroad whose products and processes can be localised in Saudi Arabia, and we help shape how that happens, so that manufacture, training, research partnerships and regulatory know-how stay in the Kingdom.
