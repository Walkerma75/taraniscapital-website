Manufacturing is the least developed part of Saudi Arabia's biotech plan, and the part moving fastest.

Until recently, local production meant generic drugs. The first biopharmaceutical plants are now being built. Lifera, which is owned by the Public Investment Fund, aims to supply more than half of the Kingdom's insulin through its subsidiary SaudiBio, with Novo Nordisk insulin filled and finished at its plant in Sudair. Also in Sudair, a SAR 500 million human vaccine facility was reported in October to be 85% complete, with the aim of meeting up to 80% of the Kingdom's essential vaccine needs.

These projects show that localisation is a ladder. The first rung is filling and finishing a product made elsewhere. The next is making the active substance. The last is developing and exporting products independently. Each rung needs know-how to be handed over, not only a building to be opened.

That is the test we apply. A company that wants a distributor in Riyadh is not a localisation candidate. A company that will transfer its process, train a Saudi team and work with local research partners is. We favour arrangements that leave lasting assets in the country: training programmes, manufacturing facilities, research collaborations and regulatory expertise.
