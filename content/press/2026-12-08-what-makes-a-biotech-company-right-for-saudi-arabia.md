Most of the international biotech companies that approach the Gulf are looking for capital or a sales channel. Very few are ready to localise. These are the five questions we use to tell the difference.

1. **Is the science already proven?** We look at established platforms, not early research. Localisation needs a product and a process that already work.
2. **Can the process be transferred?** Some manufacturing can be taught and repeated in a new country. Some depends on a handful of people in one building.
3. **Is there Saudi demand?** The strongest cases answer a health need the Kingdom has already named, where the public system is the natural customer.
4. **Will the company commit to capability?** That means a local team, training, and partnerships with Saudi universities and hospitals.
5. **Does it meet our ethics criteria?** We assess patient safety, environmental impact, regulatory compliance and equitable access alongside the commercial case.

Our scientific advisers review the science. We review the commercial case, the governance and the localisation plan. A company has to pass both.

This year we have taken a group of international companies through that review. The pattern is consistent: the science is rarely the obstacle. The obstacle is whether the company has thought through what it will leave behind in the Kingdom.
